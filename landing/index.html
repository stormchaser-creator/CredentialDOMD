Frequently asked questions

Questions before you start

Can I sign up now?
CredentialDOMD is in field testing right now. The founder runs his own licenses, CME, hospital assignments, and invoices through it every day. Early access opens to the waitlist first, in order. Leave your email above and you will get one message when your spot is ready. The earliest names on the list get founding-member perks at launch.
Is CredentialDOMD for NPs and PAs?
Yes. CredentialDOMD is for physicians (MD and DO), nurse practitioners and physician assistants. You choose your profession when you set up your account, and the app tracks the licenses and certifications that go with it: PA licenses and NCCPA certification for PAs, and APRN and RN licenses and national certification (such as AANPCB or ANCC) for NPs. The free state renewal guides on this site cover physician licenses; NP and PA requirements differ by board.
Is my credential data secure?
Your records and uploaded documents are stored with Supabase. Account access rules are designed to separate users’ records, and the operator has administrative access for support and running the service. AI features send the content you submit to Google or Anthropic; shared service keys are available, and your own key is optional. Private notes use separate browser storage and do not sync automatically. We make no HIPAA compliance claim and do not offer a BAA. Read the data-handling details and limits before uploading sensitive material.
What happens to my records if your servers go down?
You keep working. The app is offline first: your records live in your own browser as well as on the server, so a flight, a hospital basement or an outage at our end does not stop you opening a licence number or logging a case. Changes queue on the device and sync when the connection returns.

Behind that there are three separate copies. The database is Postgres at Supabase in the United States, which takes its own daily snapshot. Every night at 03:10 a second job runs on hardware we control and pulls a full copy: every table, the schema, and every document you have uploaded, into one archive encrypted with AES-256 before it is written to disk. That archive is kept for 30 days locally and copied off site for 14. Both copies carry a checksum so a corrupted file is detected rather than discovered during a restore.

The reason the nightly job exists is specific: the provider’s own snapshots cover the database but not your uploaded files. Without it, a lost project would mean lost documents. With it, the documents sit in a second place, in our custody, encrypted.
Has the restore actually been tested, or just the backup?
Tested. A backup nobody has restored is a hope, not a failsafe. The archive has been decrypted and loaded into a clean Postgres database end to end, table counts and row hashes compared against the live database, and a document opened from the restored copy. That result is written down, and the runbook puts the next full restore test on the calendar quarterly.

An archive can also be opened without us and without Supabase: it is a plain tar file behind standard OpenSSL encryption, holding your data as JSON and CSV and your documents as the original files. There is no proprietary format to be locked into and no tool of ours you need in order to read it.
Can I hold my own copy, and what if you disappear?
Yes, and you should. More > Data & Backup exports everything you have entered whenever you want it, in open formats. Once a month the server also builds a ZIP of your records and your uploaded documents and emails you that it is ready. The email carries no link to the file: you download it while signed in, through a link that expires in 15 minutes, and the three most recent months are kept.

Nothing about your record depends on this company continuing to exist. It is your licences, your CME and your documents, in formats a spreadsheet and a PDF reader can open, one export away at any time.
Does this work for my state?
Yes. CredentialDOMD supports all 50 states plus DC, Guam, Puerto Rico, and the US Virgin Islands. Each state's specific licensing requirements, CME mandates, and renewal cycles are built into the system. If you practice across multiple states, we track each one independently.
Can my practice manager use this for our whole group?
That is the plan. A practice tier with a team-wide compliance view, per-provider profiles, and consolidated alerts is on the roadmap after individual early access opens. Join the early-access list and mention your group size; practices get priority onboarding when the team features ship.
What about CAQH?
CredentialDOMD complements CAQH, it does not replace it. We track the same credential data CAQH requires, so when it is time to update your CAQH profile, everything you need is already organized and current. Think of us as the source of truth that makes CAQH updates a five-minute task instead of a two-hour ordeal.
How does the AI document scanner work?
Take a photo of any credential document: medical license, DEA registration, board certificate, insurance card. Our Gemini-powered AI reads the document, extracts the credential type, issuing authority, license number, issue date, and expiration date, then populates your profile automatically. You review and confirm. It takes about 10 seconds per document.
Can I use this offline?
You can open cached credential records and log work without a connection after signing in on that device. Changes sync when you reconnect. AI features and files that are not already available on the device need an internet connection. Private notes stay on the device unless you export and restore them yourself.
What if I only have a few credentials?
Honestly? If you have two credentials that renew on the same date, a calendar reminder might be enough. But most physicians, NPs and PAs are carrying twenty-plus (licenses, DEA registrations, board certs, privileges, titers, TB tests), each on its own cycle. If you credential at more than one facility or hold more than one state license, this is built for you. One missed renewal costs more than years of any tool.
