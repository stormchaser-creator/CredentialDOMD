i Deadlines, fees, and what happens if you are late

When it is due
MD: January 31 of every even-numbered year, regardless of when the license was issued (current period February 1, 2026 to January 31, 2028). DO: June 30 of every even-numbered year (current period July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2028). Government-employed physicians (MDG) renew annually by January 31 of every year.
Fee
$402.00 (Physician MD, on-time 2-year renewal, as posted by the board for the Feb 1, 2026 to Jan 31, 2028 period). Osteopathic Physician (DOS): $312.00. Physician-Government (MDG, annual): $171.00.
Late fee
There is no posted flat late fee; Hawaii has no late-renewal window. A license not renewed by the expiration date is forfeited, and the board instead posts a total "Cost to Restore (late renewal)": MD $408.00, DOS $392.00, MDG $271.00.
If you miss the deadline
No grace period. The board states licenses not renewed by the license expiration date are unlicensed and may not practice. A forfeited MD or DO license may be restored within 2 years of expiration (MDG within 1 year) by requesting a restoration form, submitting it with a wet signature, the restoration fee, and documentation proving CE compliance for the previous biennium. After the restoration window the license is terminated and the physician must apply as a new applicant under current requirements.

1 Step-by-Step: Renewing Your Hawaii Medical License

Follow these steps to renew your medical license with the Hawaii Medical Board (Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs, Professional and Vocational Licensing Division):

  1. Complete your CME for the cycle (40 hours) (see details below)
  2. Log in to the Hawaii Medical Board (Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs, Professional and Vocational Licensing Division) renewal portal
  3. Complete the renewal application and answer the attestation questions
  4. Pay the renewal fee ($402)
  5. Submit and save your confirmation

2 Hawaii CME Requirements

40 hours per 2-year cycle, including at least 40 Category 1.

3 Common Pitfalls to Avoid

These are the mistakes Hawaii physicians most commonly make during renewal:

  • The MD and DO deadlines are different even though one board licenses both: MDs expire January 31 of even years, DOs expire June 30 of even years. A dual-degree assumption of one date can forfeit a license.
  • There is no grace period at all. On February 1 (or July 1 for DOs) an unrenewed license is forfeited and practicing on it is unlicensed practice; recovery requires a restoration form with a wet (ink) signature plus CE proof, not just an online click.
  • Physicians employed by the government hold a separate MDG license that renews every single year by January 31 at $171, not biennially. Moving between government and private practice changes your renewal calendar.
  • Restoration is only available for 2 years after expiration (1 year for MDG). Miss that window and the license is terminated; you start over as a new applicant under current licensing requirements.
  • Renewal CE is attestation-based, but the board runs random post-renewal CME audits (audit notices for MD, DO, PO, PA, EMT are posted on the board site), so keep certificates for the 40 Category 1 hours from the prior biennium.

4 Frequently Asked Questions

When does my Hawaii MD license expire? ▾
January 31 of every even-numbered year, regardless of when the license was issued. The current cycle runs February 1, 2026 through January 31, 2028, so the next renewal window opens in late 2027 (for the 2026 renewal, forms mailed November 7, 2025 and renewals were accepted November 10, 2025 to January 31, 2026).
How do I renew online? ▾
Through the MyPVL licensee dashboard at mypvl.dcca.hawaii.gov. You sign up or log in with a myHawaii account; from the dashboard you can renew or restore an eligible license, update your address during renewal, print a copy of the renewal, and print your pocket ID card and wall certificate.
How much does renewal cost? ▾
The board posts the on-time cost to renew for the 2026-2028 period as $402.00 for a Physician (MD), $312.00 for an Osteopathic Physician (DOS), and $171.00 for the annually renewed Physician-Government (MDG) license.
What happens if I miss the January 31 deadline? ▾
The license is forfeited immediately; you are unlicensed and may not practice. You may restore within 2 years (MD/DO) or 1 year (MDG) by requesting a restoration form and submitting it with a wet signature, the restoration fee (MD $408, DOS $392, MDG $271 as posted), and documentation of CE compliance for the previous biennial cycle. After that window the license terminates and you must reapply as a new applicant.
I am a DO. Is my renewal the same as an MD's? ▾
Same board, different schedule and fee. Osteopathic physicians renew by June 30 of every even-numbered year (current period July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2028) at $312.00, with renewals accepted May 4 to June 30, 2026. Hawaii has no separate osteopathic board.
Do I have to prove my CME at renewal? ▾
Renewal is attestation-based, but the board conducts random continuing-education audits after renewal (notice-of-audit letters for MDs, DOs and other license types are posted on the board's CE and Random Audit Information page). Hawaii requires 40 Category 1 credits per biennium for MDs, so retain your certificates.
