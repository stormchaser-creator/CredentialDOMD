i Deadlines, fees, and what happens if you are late

When it is due
October 31 of each odd-numbered year, for all physicians statewide (Wis. Stat. 440.08(2)). The board states this applies even if the initial license was issued in the months just before the date: a license issued shortly before October 31 of an odd year still must be renewed by that date. Next renewal deadline: October 31, 2027.
Fee
$120 (Medicine and Surgery, MD or DO, 2-year renewal). Same $120 applies to the Compact (IMLC-issued) MD and DO credentials and to Administrative Medicine and Surgery licenses. Per the DSPS Renewal Dates and Fees schedule (RDAF, rev. 08/07/2025); DSPS's August 8, 2025 news release confirms the physician fee rose to $120 effective for applications opening in August 2025.
Late fee
$25 late renewal fee, making the total $145 with the renewal fee (Wis. Stat. 440.08(3)(a); RDAF schedule shows $145 "with late fee" for MD and DO).
If you miss the deadline
No practice grace period: the license expires October 31 if no renewal was filed. However, under Wis. Stat. 227.51(2) (quoted on the DSPS physician page), if the renewal application and fee are submitted by 11:59 pm on the deadline, the existing license remains valid until DSPS finishes acting on the application. After expiration, the credential can be restored at any time by paying the renewal fee plus the $25 late fee (Wis. Stat. 440.08(3)(a)). If expired more than 5 years, the board may impose additional restoration requirements to establish competency, not to exceed initial-licensure education/exam requirements (Wis. Stat. 440.08(3)(b)). DSPS may also summarily deny a renewal to protect public health, safety, or welfare, with a 30-day window to request a hearing (Wis. Stat. 440.08(4)).
Processing time
DSPS does not publish a physician renewal processing time on its site. The board page notes that a timely-filed renewal keeps the license valid while under review (Wis. Stat. 227.51(2)), and employers can confirm active status via DSPS Public Look-Up. At a September 30, 2025 DSPS renewal webinar (reported by the Wisconsin Hospital Association), DSPS said legal file reviews take about one week once all requested information is received.

1 Step-by-Step: Renewing Your Wisconsin Medical License

Follow these steps to renew your medical license with the Wisconsin Medical Examining Board (Department of Safety and Professional Services, DSPS):

  1. Complete your CME for the cycle (30 hours) (see details below)
  2. Log in to the Wisconsin Medical Examining Board (Department of Safety and Professional Services, DSPS) renewal portal
  3. Complete the renewal application and answer the attestation questions
  4. Pay the renewal fee ($120 (Medicine and Surgery, MD or DO, 2-year renewal))
  5. Submit and save your confirmation

2 Wisconsin CME Requirements

30 hours per 2-year cycle. at least 30 Category 1, 2 hrs opioid prescribing.

3 Common Pitfalls to Avoid

These are the mistakes Wisconsin physicians most commonly make during renewal:

  • The renewal date is fixed at October 31 of odd years no matter when the license was issued. DSPS states explicitly that a physician first licensed in the months leading up to the date must still renew by that date, so a new licensee can owe a full renewal within weeks of initial licensure.
  • IMLC (Interstate Medical Licensure Compact) licensees do not renew through the Wisconsin LicensE portal; DSPS directs them to renew at imlcc.com/renew. Renewing in the wrong system stalls the renewal.
  • Renewal holds tied to the Office of the Commissioner of Insurance (typically Injured Patients and Families Compensation Fund compliance) blocked some 2025 renewals; DSPS addressed these at its September 30, 2025 webinar. Resolve any OCI/fund issue before the October 31 deadline rather than waiting for DSPS to clear it.
  • For the 2025 cycle, CME was attested at renewal, not uploaded: DSPS said CE Broker would not be used for the renewal itself but would be used for post-renewal audits. Physicians must retain CME completion evidence through the biennium because the Medical Examining Board runs random audits (Wis. Admin. Code MED ch. 13).
  • The renewal addendum (Form 4570) asks health, substance use, discipline, DEA, and malpractice-payout questions scoped to what has 'not been previously disclosed.' A yes answer, or an incomplete addendum, routes the file to legal review and delays the renewal, so gather supporting documentation before starting.

4 Frequently Asked Questions

When is my Wisconsin medical license renewal due? ▾
October 31 of every odd-numbered year, for every MD and DO regardless of when the license was first issued (Wis. Stat. 440.08(2); DSPS physician page). The next deadline is October 31, 2027.
How much does renewal cost? ▾
$120 for the 2-year renewal for both MDs and DOs, per the DSPS Renewal Dates and Fees schedule (rev. 08/07/2025). DSPS raised the fee to $120 in August 2025 and notes it remains the lowest physician renewal fee in the nation.
Where do I renew? ▾
Online through the LicensE portal at license.wi.gov (log in, My Approved Licenses, choose Renew from the Action dropdown). Physicians licensed through the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact renew at imlcc.com/renew instead. A paper route exists via the Medicine and Surgery Renewal Addendum, Form 4570.
Can I keep practicing if DSPS has not finished processing my renewal by November 1? ▾
Yes, if you submitted the application and paid the fee by 11:59 pm on October 31. Under Wis. Stat. 227.51(2), a timely and sufficient renewal keeps the existing license valid until DSPS finally acts on it. You must stay responsive to any DSPS requests, and your employer can verify status on DSPS Public Look-Up; DSPS will not issue written confirmation.
What happens if I miss the deadline? ▾
The license expires and you cannot practice on it, but you can restore it by paying the $120 renewal fee plus a $25 late fee ($145 total) under Wis. Stat. 440.08(3)(a). If the license has been expired more than 5 years, the board may require additional competency-related requirements before restoring it (Wis. Stat. 440.08(3)(b)).
What CME do I need to attest at renewal? ▾
30 hours of Category 1 CME (AMA, AOA, or ACCME-approved) per biennium, including 2 hours on prescribing opioids and other controlled substances for physicians holding a DEA number (Wis. Admin. Code MED ch. 13). You attest at renewal and keep proof for the board's random post-renewal audits; for the 2025 cycle DSPS said CE Broker is used for audits, not for the renewal itself.
