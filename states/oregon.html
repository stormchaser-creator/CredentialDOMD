i Deadlines, fees, and what happens if you are late

When it is due
Fixed date, statewide: the renewal application and fee must be received and satisfactorily complete on or before December 31 of each odd-numbered year for all physicians and physician associates (OAR 847-008-0005(1)). All MD/DO licensees share the same deadline regardless of when the license was issued; the current registration period ends December 31, 2027. Emeritus renews by December 31 every year; acupuncturists (also under this board) renew by June 30 of even-numbered years.
Fee
$756 total for an Active MD/DO two-year renewal per the board fee schedule effective 7/2/2026: $608 license registration + $20 OHSU library + $50 HPSP fee + $70 prescription monitoring + $8 OHA workforce database. Inactive or Administrative Medicine status totals $686 (no PDMP fee). Active-One Year (postgraduate) is $378/year; Emeritus $114/year. Underlying rule: registration is $314/year including $10/year OHSU library (OAR 847-005-0005), rising to $375/year on or after March 1, 2028. All fees are nonrefundable, nontransferable, and cannot be prorated.
Late fee
$195 (Delinquent MD/DO Registration Renewal, OAR 847-005-0005(6)(a)), assessed for any renewal not submitted and paid before the December 31 deadline.
If you miss the deadline
No grace period for practice: a license not renewed by December 31 lapses and the licensee may not practice; practicing medicine on a lapsed license is practicing without a license, a felony and grounds for discipline (board renewal page). Reinstatement path (OAR 847-008-0050): within 90 days of the end of the registration period, reinstate by paying the $195 late fee plus the renewal fees and completing the required forms. After 90 days but within two biennia, a full Reactivation Application is required ($50 processing fee) plus the late fee and back renewal fees for the lapsed periods, with additional requirements under OAR 847-008-0055. A license not reinstated or reactivated within two biennia (about 4 years) expires, and the former licensee must reapply as a new applicant under current requirements.
Processing time
Not published as a fixed figure. The board asks licensees to complete renewal by December 1 so staff have time to review and process before the December 31 deadline; once approved, an email tells you how to print the new Certificate of Registration from Applicant/Licensee Services.

1 Step-by-Step: Renewing Your Oregon Medical License

Follow these steps to renew your medical license with the Oregon Medical Board:

  1. Complete your CME for the cycle (60 hours) (see details below)
  2. Log in to the Oregon Medical Board renewal portal
  3. Complete the renewal application and answer the attestation questions
  4. Pay the renewal fee ($756 total for an Active MD/DO two-year renewal per the board fee schedule effective 7/2/2026: $608 license registration + $20 OHSU library + $50 HPSP fee + $70 prescription monitoring + $8 OHA workforce database)
  5. Submit and save your confirmation

2 Oregon CME Requirements

60 hours per 2-year cycle.

  • At least 60 hours in Category 1
  • 1 hour of pain management
  • 2 hours of cultural competency

3 Common Pitfalls to Avoid

These are the mistakes Oregon physicians most commonly make during renewal:

  • No renewal notice is required for the deadline to bind you: the board states a renewal notification is not required for you to renew, so waiting for a reminder that never arrives still ends in a lapsed license on January 1.
  • There is no practice grace period: the license lapses at midnight December 31 of the odd year, and practicing on a lapsed license is a felony (practicing without a license). The 90-day late window only lets you reinstate the license, not keep practicing while lapsed.
  • Miss the 90-day reinstatement window and you are into full reactivation: application, $50 processing fee, the $195 delinquent fee, and back renewal fees for every lapsed period. Let it sit more than two biennia and the license expires outright, forcing a brand-new application (OAR 847-008-0050).
  • Fees are nonrefundable, nontransferable, and never prorated. Prescribers also pay a $70/biennium Prescription Drug Monitoring Program fee on top of registration, so the real Active renewal cost is $756, not the $628 registration line.
  • Paper renewals are not offered at all; renewal is online only through omb.oregon.gov. Also expect a possible random CME audit triggered during the renewal application, with 60 days to produce documentation (MOC participation can satisfy it).
  • A scheduled fee increase is already in rule: MD/DO registration rises from $314/year to $375/year on or after March 1, 2028, so the renewal due December 31, 2029 will cost more.

4 Frequently Asked Questions

When is my Oregon MD/DO license renewal due? ▾
On or before December 31 of each odd-numbered year, for every physician and physician associate at once (OAR 847-008-0005(1)). The current period ends December 31, 2027. The application and fee must be received and satisfactorily complete by that date; the board recommends finishing by December 1.
How much does renewal cost? ▾
For an Active MD/DO license, $756 for the two-year period under the fee schedule effective 7/2/2026 ($608 registration, $20 OHSU library, $50 HPSP, $70 prescription monitoring, $8 workforce database). Inactive is $686. Fees are nonrefundable and not prorated.
Where do I renew? ▾
Online only, at https://omb.oregon.gov/renew (Applicant/Licensee Services login at https://omb.oregon.gov/login). Paper renewals are not offered. Renewal questions: renewal@omb.oregon.gov or 971-673-2700.
What happens if I miss the December 31 deadline? ▾
The license lapses and you may not practice; practicing on a lapsed license is a felony. Within 90 days you can reinstate by paying the $195 delinquent fee plus renewal fees. After 90 days you must file a reactivation application with additional fees; after two biennia the license expires and you reapply as a new applicant.
Will I be audited for CME at renewal? ▾
Possibly. The board runs a random CME audit of renewing licensees; you are notified during the renewal application and must respond within 60 days with documentation. Ongoing maintenance of certification participation can satisfy the audit, and staff will verify it directly with the certifying board. Oregon requires 60 hours per biennium plus the Pain Management Commission module and cultural competency hours (OAR 847-008-0070).
I am leaving Oregon or not practicing here. What should I do at renewal? ▾
The board's options: renew at Inactive status if you will not work in Oregon now, or Locum Tenens if you will work intermittently (with advance written notice of each Oregon practice date and location). If you will not return, you can surrender or retire the license at the start of the renewal application, or simply let it lapse by taking no action.
