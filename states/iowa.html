i Deadlines, fees, and what happens if you are late

When it is due
License expires on the first day of the licensee's birth month; renewal issues a two-year license that again expires on the first day of the birth month (Iowa Admin. Code r. 481-652.12(3)). Online renewal opens no more than 60 days before the renewal date.
Fee
$450 if renewed online, $550 if renewed by paper application, per biennial period (prorated if the current license was issued for less than 24 months); $0 for physicians on active duty in the U.S. armed forces, reserves, or national guard during the renewal period. A $7 online transaction fee applies to online purchases (Iowa Admin. Code r. 481-651.4(1)c, 481-651.1).
Late fee
$100 per calendar month after the license expiration date, up to a maximum of $200 (Iowa Admin. Code r. 481-651.4(1)d).
If you miss the deadline
A physician who misses the expiration date may still renew during a two-month penalty window ($100/month, max $200). If not renewed by the first day of the third month after expiration, the license becomes inactive and invalid, and practice is prohibited (r. 481-652.12(4), 481-652.13(2)). Reactivation within one year of becoming inactive costs $550 and requires a renewal application plus full CME documentation (r. 481-652.14(1)). Reactivation after one year or more costs $500 plus a $45 fingerprint/background-check fee and requires a full reactivation application, 40 hours of Category 1 CME in the previous two years, and, if out of active clinical practice for 3+ years, a board-approved competency evaluation, SPEX/COMVEX, retraining, or reentry program (r. 481-652.14(2)). A license not renewed or reactivated within five years after expiration is deemed relinquished and can never be reinstated; the physician must apply for a brand-new license (r. 481-652.16).

1 Step-by-Step: Renewing Your Iowa Medical License

Follow these steps to renew your medical license with the Iowa Board of Medicine (Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing):

  1. Complete your CME for the cycle (40 hours) (see details below)
  2. Log in to the Iowa Board of Medicine (Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing) renewal portal
  3. Complete the renewal application and answer the attestation questions
  4. Pay the renewal fee ($450 if renewed online, $550 if renewed by paper application, per biennial period (prorated if the current license was issued for less than 24 months); $0 for physicians on active duty in the U)
  5. Submit and save your confirmation

2 Iowa CME Requirements

40 hours per 2-year cycle.

  • At least 40 hours in Category 1
  • 2 hours of child abuse recognition
  • 2 hours of dependent adult abuse
  • 2 hours of opioid prescribing
  • 2 hours of end-of-life care

3 Common Pitfalls to Avoid

These are the mistakes Iowa physicians most commonly make during renewal:

  • The online renewal option does not appear in the portal until 60 days before your renewal date, so you cannot knock it out months early; conversely, waiting past the first day of your birth month starts a $100-per-month penalty clock.
  • Renewing on paper costs $100 more than renewing online ($550 vs $450), and online payments carry an additional $7 transaction fee.
  • The board must complete the renewal application from the physician personally; DIAL states third-party applications (e.g., credentialing services) are not accepted for online renewal.
  • If you slip past the two-month penalty window, the license goes inactive on the first day of the third month after expiration and any practice on it is prohibited and subject to discipline, injunction, or criminal sanction (Iowa Code 147.83, 147.86). Reactivation within one year still requires the full CME documentation, and none of the credits earned while inactive can be carried over to a future license period.
  • A license left inactive for five years after expiration is deemed relinquished permanently; there is no reinstatement path at that point, only a full new-license application. And after one year inactive, reactivation adds fingerprinting/background check, and 3+ years out of clinical practice triggers SPEX/COMVEX or a board-approved competency or reentry program.

4 Frequently Asked Questions

When does my Iowa medical license expire and how often do I renew? ▾
Every two years. The permanent (and administrative medicine) license expires on the first day of your birth month, and renewal issues another two-year license with the same birth-month anchor (Iowa Admin. Code r. 481-652.12(3)).
How early can I renew online? ▾
The renew option in the board's AMANDA portal becomes available no more than 60 days before your renewal date. You must complete the application yourself; third-party submissions are not accepted.
How much does renewal cost? ▾
$450 online or $550 by paper per biennial period, prorated if your current license was issued for under 24 months. Online purchases add a $7 transaction fee. Physicians on active duty in the U.S. armed forces, reserves, or national guard during the renewal period owe no renewal fee (r. 481-651.4(1)c).
What happens if I miss my expiration date? ▾
You can still renew for up to two months with a penalty of $100 per calendar month (max $200). On the first day of the third month after expiration the license becomes inactive and invalid, and you may not practice until it is reactivated (r. 481-652.12(4), 481-651.4(1)d).
My license went inactive. How do I get it back? ▾
Within one year of going inactive: submit a renewal application, the $550 reinstatement fee, and documentation of the CME and mandatory training for the most recent period. After one year or more: a full reactivation application, $500 plus $45 fingerprint/background check, 40 hours of Category 1 CME within the previous two years, and if you have not practiced clinically in 3+ years, a board-approved competency evaluation, SPEX/COMVEX, retraining, or reentry program (r. 481-652.14).
Do MDs and DOs renew with the same board? ▾
Yes. Iowa has no separate osteopathic board; the Iowa Board of Medicine licenses both medicine and surgery and osteopathic medicine and surgery under Iowa Code chapter 148, with identical renewal rules and fees.
