The form is not the problem

Two signups from two attempts means everyone who tried, succeeded. There is no leak to plug. The funnel was rebuilt this week and the analytics went in yesterday, so from here the question is entirely traffic: getting a physician in front of a page that already works.

Calibrate to this curve. Week 1 produces effectively zero organic traffic; Search Console shows impressions before clicks. Long-tail pages like the state guides typically start ranking in one to three months, with consistent movement at three to six. The compounding window in every comparable case study is months six to eighteen. Quitting the guides early is the one guaranteed way to lose that.

Measurement

What we watch, in order

Signup totals are explicitly not the week-to-week success metric until month three. These are, in decision-value order.

SignalWhy it leads
Search Console impressions and position, by guide pageThe leading indicator for the entire SEO asset. Moves before clicks do.
Page views by path, with referrerCommunity and social activity shows here as referrer and direct spikes long before it shows in signups.
Guide-email capture rate per pageThe funnel's actual conversion number. This gates any future sponsorship spend.
Leads by source tagEvery link you post carries ?src=. Where a venue bans links, attribution falls back to referrer and time-correlated spikes, logged honestly as estimates.

Monday review, 20 minutes. Each channel gets one of three verdicts: double down, hold, or kill. Kill rule is four consecutive weeks of zero signal.

Non-negotiable

Rules of engagement

The disclosure line, every time"I built this. Founder of CredentialDOMD." The FTC treats founder equity as a material connection requiring clear disclosure regardless of company size, and AMA ethics guidance says the same for physicians. No platform is exempt.
No invented numbers, everWith two users the honest line is "small invite-only beta". The $47,000 figure, the fabricated testimonials, and the "true story" privilege lapse in the old drafts are dead unless you personally confirm them real.
No compliance claimsNot "HIPAA compliant", not "bank-level encryption". Only literal, verifiable design facts: no patient data enters the product, keys stay on your device. Henry Schein paid $250,000 and took a 20-year FTC consent order for exactly this category of claim.
Price discipline$199 a year, flat. Never "cheaper than Mocingbird", because it is exact price parity. The argument is always more product for the same price.
Tax content is record-keeping, never adviceFramed as estimation and record-keeping, citing IRS publications. Never individualized.
